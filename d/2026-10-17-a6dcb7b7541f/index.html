⛩️ Japanese rokuyo

Hidori Calendar (ohigara.com), 2026年10月17日（土）:

“2026年10月17日（土）の六曜は「先負」です。”
English: The rokuyo for Saturday, October 17, 2026 is “Senbu” (also read Sakimake).
“「先んずれば負ける」。午前は凶・午後は吉とされ、午後に落ち着いて動くのが良いとされます。”
English: “If you go first, you lose.” The morning is considered unlucky and the afternoon lucky; it is said to be best to act calmly in the afternoon.
十二直 (twelve-day cycle): 満（みつ）: “万物が満ち足りる日。祭祀・移転・婚礼・建築に良いとされます。服薬や土を動かすことは避けるとされます。”
English: Mitsu (“Full”): a day when all things are filled and satisfied. Considered good for rituals, moving house, weddings and construction; starting medicine and moving earth are said to be avoided.
吉日 (lucky-day mark): 天恩日: “天の恩恵を受けるとされる吉日。祝い事には良いが、弔事には用いないとされます。”
English: Ten'onnichi: a lucky day said to receive heaven's blessings. Good for celebrations, but not used for funerals.
Other notes: “日の干支「甲子」（60干支のうち1番目）· 二十八宿「氐」· 月齢 6.5 · この日に当たる凶日とされる暦注はありません。”
English: Day stem-branch “Kinoe-Ne” (Wood Rat), the 1st of the 60-day cycle · lunar mansion “Tei” · Moon age 6.5 days · There are no unlucky almanac marks on this day.

The site itself notes: 「六曜・選日は科学的根拠のない民間の暦注です。」 English: “Rokuyo and selected days are folk almanac notes with no scientific basis.” Japan is 13 hours ahead of ET, so Japan's “lucky afternoon” (noon to midnight JST) falls between 11 PM Friday and 11 AM Saturday ET. Most people simply apply it to their own local afternoon (my note, not the site's).

What it means: Senbu is the “don't rush” day of Japan's six-day luck cycle. Hurrying, competing or pushing urgent business is discouraged in the morning, and calm action after noon is favored. It also carries the “Full” mark and a heavenly-blessing (Ten'onnichi) mark, with no unlucky marks, so overall it's a mild, good-natured day if you take it slowly.

In short: Don't jump first. Ease through the morning and do your real moves in the afternoon; otherwise it's a blessed, unlucky-mark-free day.
